# Understanding the risks

Our municipal investments are not a savings account. That means there are risks when you invest, but
there is a very low risk of the council failing to repay you, due to the strict financial rules that
councils operate under.

## What’s the risk of losing your money?

When you invest, it is important to consider whether scheduled payments will be made, and if your
original investment will be repaid.

As a potential investor in a council, media headlines around council finances - such as multi-million
pound budget shortfalls and concerns about ‘bankruptcy’ - might be alarming to read. But these
headlines do not reflect the reality when it comes to the risk of a council not paying you back.

Indeed, there is no record of a UK council ever defaulting on a debt, and there have been no defaults
on our municipal investments. This is because of the way that councils, and their finances, are
regulated.

### Key points to understand

#### Councils have to repay their loans

Councils cannot be declared bankrupt and leave unpaid creditors behind, like companies can. They
have to repay loans made to them even if they get into financial trouble.

#### Councils operate under strict financial controls

Because of their critical role in our society, councils are legally required to operate balanced
budgets. This includes budgeting for repayments on any money they borrow, which means they can only
take out new borrowing they can afford to repay.

#### Councils have clear processes to follow if they get into financial trouble

If a council gets into financial trouble there is a specified process they must follow (called
issuing a Section 114 notice in England). This allows them to take additional steps to balance their
budgets. But, crucially for investors, they are not allowed to use this process to avoid paying
loans, or interest owed - even in the most severe cases, when central government becomes involved.

### Key risks of municipal investments

Fixed term and interest rate

The investments are long term - usually five years - with rates fixed for the whole term. Check you are happy with this before you invest, remembering that interest rates on other financial products may go up and down during the investment term.

You might not get all your money back if you sell early

We operate a marketplace if you want to sell before the term ends. But there is no guarantee you will find a buyer at the price you wish to sell (particularly if interest rates on other financial products have changed during the investment term). Learn more

No FSCS protection for investments

In relation to claims against failed regulated firms, the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) does not cover investments in P2P loans like our council investments. Learn more in our helpcentre

### Investment risk FAQs

Is the risk different when I invest through an IF ISA?

No. If you choose to invest through an ISA, it does not change the risk of the investment.
It only means that any interest from your investment will be tax free.

---

What happens if Abundance goes out of business?

If Abundance were to go out of business, the administration of your investment could be
disrupted and you may be prevented from selling your investment. We have plans in place to
ensure the payments on your investments continue to be administered. Read more

---

Is a municipal investment the same as a savings account?

No. A savings account is a deposit product. A municipal investment is an investment where
you lend money to a council.

---

Can a council go bankrupt?

Councils do not go bankrupt in the same way companies can. If a council gets into serious
financial difficulty, it may issue a Section 114 notice, which restricts new spending while
it works to balance its budget. This does not allow the council to walk away from existing
loan repayments.

---

Can I sell my investment before the end of the term?

You can try to sell through our marketplace, but selling is not guaranteed. You may have to
accept less than you originally invested, particularly if market interest rates have
changed.
