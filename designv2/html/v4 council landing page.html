How your money can help

Money raised through H&F Green Investment helps support practical local projects that contribute to a cleaner, greener and more resilient borough.

1

Cut carbon from local buildings

Funding can support improvements that reduce emissions, improve efficiency and help public buildings perform better over time.

2

Improve neighbourhood spaces

Investment can help deliver greener streets, better public realm and projects that make local places healthier and more enjoyable to use.

3

Back visible local action

Rather than funding something abstract, investors can support projects with tangible local outcomes that residents can recognise.

Hammersmith & Fulham Council is at the forefront of the green finance revolution. Investors know that their money is being invested in projects that will make a positive impact on our communities, and people’s lives.

Meanwhile, it means that the council can deliver climate and ecological projects cost-effectively, which will help save taxpayer money. It’s a rare win-win-win.

Rowan Ree

Cllr Rowan Ree

A practical route into local projects

This investment is designed to connect people more directly with the places they care about. It helps the council raise money from citizens and supporters in a way that is visible, understandable and rooted in real local outcomes.

That means investment can support projects that residents recognise, rather than disappearing into something abstract or distant.

Investment connected to place

One of the strengths of this model is that it makes local authority borrowing more visible. Investors are not only receiving a fixed return — they are backing projects rooted in a real borough, with outcomes people can recognise.

That gives the page an important second role: helping explain what is being financed and why that matters locally.

Key terms

H&F Green Investment 5

Full key terms for the live investment opportunity.

Borrower Hammersmith & Fulham Council
Use of funds Funding Eligible Green Projects under the Green Finance Framework
Interest rate 4.1% a year
This is the interest rate paid by the Local Authority to lenders and does not include the cost of fees paid to Abundance in its role as Arranger and Agent.
Term period 5 years after end of 'Initial interest period'
Maturity date 31 March 2031
Capital repayment Lump sum on maturity
Offer open date 1 December 2025
Offer close date 2 March 2026
The Loan Opportunity may be withdrawn before the Offer Close Date at the Borrower's discretion subject to it posting notice of withdrawal on the Abundance website.
Minimum borrowing amount £5.00
Maximum borrowing amount There is no formal Maximum Borrowing Amount. The Borrower may raise such amount as agreed with Abundance in the context of its wider borrowing limits.
Initial interest period This will start on the date the lender makes their investment and ends on 31 March 2026.
Interest periods From 1 April to 30 September and 1 October to 31 March each year, starting from 1 April 2026.
Return structure 10 semi-annual payments of Interest on the last day of each Interest Period. Capital is repaid as a lump sum on the Maturity Date.
Set up Fee 0.75% of total amount raised
This is the fee paid to Abundance in its role as Arranger. The interest rate above is quoted after fees.
Administration Fee 0.20% of total amount raised per year
This is the fee paid to Abundance in its role as Agent. The interest rate above is quoted after fees.
Early repayment options The Borrower has the option to make a full early repayment of the Loans by giving notice to Abundance. An early repayment fee equal to 12 months’ Interest will apply.
