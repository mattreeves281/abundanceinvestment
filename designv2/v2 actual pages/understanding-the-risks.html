Understanding the risks

Our municipal investments are not a savings account. That means there are risks when you invest, but there is a very low risk of the council failing to repay you, due to the strict financial rules that councils operate under.

What’s the risk of losing your money?

When you invest, it is important to consider whether scheduled payments will be made, and if your original investment will be repaid.

As a potential investor in a council, media headlines around council finances - such as multi-million pound budget shortfalls and concerns about ‘bankruptcy’ - might be alarming to read. But these headlines do not reflect the reality when it comes to the risk of a council not paying you back.

Indeed, there is no record of a UK council ever defaulting on a debt, and there have been no defaults on our municipal investments. This is because of the way that councils, and their finances, are regulated.

Key points to understand

Councils have to repay their loans

Councils cannot be declared bankrupt and leave unpaid creditors behind, like companies can. They have to repay loans made to them even if they get into financial trouble.

Councils operate under strict financial controls

Because of their critical role in our society, councils are legally required to operate balanced budgets. This includes budgeting for repayments on any money they borrow, which means they can only take out new borrowing they can afford to repay.

Councils have clear processes to follow if they get into financial trouble

If a council gets into financial trouble there is a specified process they must follow (called issuing a Section 114 notice in England). This allows them to take additional steps to balance their budgets. But, crucially for investors, they are not allowed to use this process to avoid paying loans, or interest owed - even in the most severe cases, when central government becomes involved.

Key risks of municipal investments

1

Fixed term and interest rate

The investments are long term - usually five years - with rates fixed for the whole term. Check you are happy with this before you invest, remembering that interest rates on other financial products may go up and down during the investment term.

2

You might not get all your money back if you sell early

We operate a marketplace if you want to sell before the term ends. But there is no guarantee you will find a buyer at the price you wish to sell (particularly if interest rates on other financial products have changed during the investment term). Learn more

3

No FSCS protection for investments

In relation to claims against failed regulated firms, the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) does not cover investments in P2P loans like our council investments. Learn more in our helpcentre
