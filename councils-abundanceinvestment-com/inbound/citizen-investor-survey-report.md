# Citizen investors say municipal investments build trust, partnership and local engagement

Abundance's latest survey of municipal investors suggests that citizen investment programmes do more than raise capital. They also create a practical way for councils to build trust, strengthen relationships with residents and show the public what is being delivered in their place.

The survey, carried out in August 2026, asked investors about the wider impact of investing in a Community Municipal Investment (CMI). The results below focus on investors who had invested in their own council, or in a council they had a connection to.

The headline finding is not simply that investors like the idea of municipal investing. It is that many investors say the experience changes how they think about councils, local delivery and their own role in the future of their place.

## Trust through delivery

Trust is often treated as a communications challenge. The survey points to something more practical: people respond when they can see what is being delivered and how it connects to them.

Municipal investments give councils a way to make that connection tangible. A resident can invest in a named programme, understand the projects being financed and receive updates as the work progresses. The investment creates a direct line between a council's plans and a citizen's stake in those plans.

[Quote block: Abundance interpretation on delivery, trust and investor updates.]

[Pull stat/card: 55.0% trust councils that issue a CMI more than those that do not. 47.9% trust their council more knowing it is delivering projects from their investment.]

For councils, this is the central trust-building opportunity. The model does not rely on abstract claims about ambition or place. It gives residents a concrete reason to pay attention to what is being delivered locally.

## Partnership and participation

The survey also suggests that municipal investments can shift the relationship between councils and citizens. More than half of connected investors said they felt their council was working in partnership with citizens or residents.

That matters because many local projects are long term by nature. They involve infrastructure, climate action, buildings, public spaces and community assets. These are the kinds of decisions that shape daily life, but they can feel remote from most residents. A citizen investment programme gives people a direct and understandable way to take part.

[Quote block: "[I invested because I am] funding initiatives that mean something to me in a place that means something to me."]

[Quote block: "I value the opportunity to help improve the places and make them healthier and more sustainable."]

The qualitative responses reinforce the point. Investors talked about supporting practical climate action, backing local leadership and wanting to see councils find new ways to fund community and green projects. The language is not only financial. It is about belonging, agency and visible local progress.

[Quote block: Abundance interpretation on CMIs as a partnership-building tool.]

## Engagement with local government

One of the quieter findings is that municipal investments appear to increase interest in local government. Investors reported feeling more connected to their community and taking more interest in local government after investing.

This is important because councils often need to explain complex, long-term investment decisions in a difficult public environment. A CMI creates a reason to tell that story clearly: what is being financed, why it matters and how residents will see the benefit.

[Quote block: "Local councils do much more than just empty the bins and fill the pot holes - investing is a way to show you support the long term investment that is needed for any community to thrive."]

In that sense, the investment is also a narrative device. It gives councils a structured way to talk about delivery over time, with an audience that has chosen to be involved.

## What this means for councils

Municipal investments are designed as a cost-effective borrowing tool, but the survey shows the wider impact can be just as important. By raising capital from citizens, councils can build a broader story around local delivery.

The evidence from investors is clear. When councils give people a tangible way to support local investment, many respond with greater trust, interest and connection. For councils looking to strengthen public understanding of what they deliver, that is a powerful starting point.

*Source: Abundance "Wider Impact of Municipal Investments" survey, August 2026. Figures shown are for investors who had invested in their own council, or a council they had a connection to. n=140.*
