# What are municipal investments?

Our municipal investments are loans to local authorities from citizens and mission-aligned institutional investors. They are designed to be cost-effective and simple to operate, and fit within your existing treasury management processes.

// card: simple card with bulleted content inside

## card: Municipal investments at a glance

- Loans are treated in the same way as other long-term borrowing
- They can only be used to finance capital expenditure
- All loans are accredited, so funds can only be used for eligible projects
- The low cost of operating online and using a templated process means the cost of funds undercuts PWLB (including all Abundance fees)
- All administration is managed by the Abundance platform
- Raise targets underwritten by mission-aligned institutions

# Accreditation

It is important for investors to have confidence in how the funds they invest will be used. This is why we accredit all our municipal investments as Green or Social loans.

We use the Green & Social Loan Principles to govern this process. These principles were developed by the Loan Market Association, along with other international finance associations, to strengthen the integrity of the market by providing a consistent framework. The UK Government has adopted the same approach, using the sameGreen Loan Principles to underpin its Green Gilt programme.

For municipal investments, verification and accreditation is performed by Abundance. We have designed the process to work within councils’ standard operating model, and it has been reviewed by the Loan Market Association for general compliance.

## The four core components of accreditation

### Use of funds

The cornerstone of accreditation is how funds will be used. Intended projects must fall within a pre-determined list of categories to qualify as eligible projects (see below). Projects may be new, or a re-finance of an existing project delivered 12 months prior to launch date of the loan.

### Project evaluation and selection

Any project in a council’s capital programme can be funded as long as it falls within one of the defined categories. As well defining the project type, the council should clearly communicate its process for evaluating projects, as well as their green, social or sustainable objectives. 

### Management of funds

Funds should be tracked by the council in an appropriate manner and documented using a formal internal process. The documentation must be linked to the delivery of the eligible projects, thereby ensuring that all funds raised are allocated to the relevant project.

### Reporting and verification

Councils should provide regular reports (via Abundance) to investors on how funds have been used. These should include details of the projects funded, the amounts allocated and, wherever possible, their expected impact.

## Four principles for growing your resident investor community

### Think long term

It takes time to build awareness. A 5-year raise target with a programme of loans will boost confidence that this is a long-term initiative and worthy of consideration as part of citizens’ financial plans, and not a one-off.

### Be consistent

Regular raises - 2 to 4 times a year - build familiarity and give citizens the chance to plan around their own savings and investment goals.

### Plan regular communications

It is important to let citizens know about your programme. Our experience suggests the best strategy for awareness is regular mentions planned across a variety of different communication channels. 

### Consider project storytelling

Strong storytelling through project selection and ongoing reporting helps to build engagement with citizens. You can focus on landmark projects, but we find it is equally valuable to shine a light on the initiatives that may go unnoticed by citizens. So far we have found that either approach can be effective for engaging citizens.

# FAQs

## What are the costs?

Abundance takes a set up fee for each successful raise - a percentage of the raise amount. We also charge an ongoing administration fee which, which is a percentage of outstanding capital. The administration fee is charged periodically across the year, combining fees from all outstanding loans.

Abundance ensures the total cost of capital (i.e. interest rate to investors and fees paid) is lower than the PWLB certainty rate, ensuring municipal investments provide the council with value for money. 

## What projects are most attractive to investors?

Specific project selection is not a driver of success. Investors want to know the council has a plan for the improvement of their place, and understand how the projects fit into that plan. There is value in using the investment process to shine a light on projects which are less visible, as well as giving people the chance to be involved in delivering high-profile projects.

The model has been used to finance everything from pool covers, cycleway improvements, flood defence and ground-mounted solar farms.

## Are there any additional audit requirements?

As this is not a listed investment there is no additional audit requirement.

## What constitutes a default?

Terms of what would constitute a default are within the standard loan conditions. In short however, the main “Events of Default” to consider are:

- Failure to pay interest and/or capital repayments as per the debt service schedule and as described in the loan documentation within five working days of when it is due.
- Any material representation, warranty or undertaking given by the borrower has been found to be misleading and is not rectified within 20 business days.
- An appointment of a receiver to administer the borrower or a procedure similar in effect.
- It becomes unlawful for the borrower to continue to comply with the conditions of the loans.

If an Event of Default occurs and is continuing, Abundance, as agent of the Lenders, may by notice in writing declare all amounts accrued or outstanding under the Loans to be immediately due and payable.

## Can we repay an investment early?

A borrower may repay early by giving 30 business days’ notice of its intention to do so. The borrower must repay the principal amount outstanding, interest accrued up to the day on which the early repayment is made (interest is calculated on a days-in-year basis, i.e. the number of days since the last interest payment divided by 365) and an “Early Repayment Fee.” This is calculated as 12 months’ worth of interest beyond the day on which the early repayment is made.

## What are the regulatory obligations?

Abundance Investment is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Council (our firm reference is 525432). Our role is to ensure the offer is made in compliance with all relevant FCA rules. The council’s obligation is to be open and honest with Abundance to enable Abundance to ensure that the offer is made in compliance with the rules.

## Can a municipal investment be blended with other sources of borrowing?

Yes, we expect councils to blend borrowing sources while they are growing their community of lenders. If a project requires a larger amount of finance than can currently be raised through a Municipal Investment, the council can borrow additional finance from PWLB or other traditional lenders to supplement funds raised from their citizens.
