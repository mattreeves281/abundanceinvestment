What are municipal investments?

Our municipal investments are loans to local authorities from citizens and mission-aligned institutional investors. They are designed to be cost-effective and simple to operate, and to fit within your existing treasury management processes.

Municipal investments at a glance

  • Loans are treated in the same way as other long-term borrowing
  • They can only be used to finance capital expenditure
  • All loans are accredited, so funds can only be used for eligible projects
  • The low cost of operating online and using a templated process means the cost of funds undercuts PWLB (including all Abundance fees)
  • All administration is managed by the Abundance platform
  • Raise targets underwritten by mission-aligned institutions

Accreditation

It is important for investors to have confidence in how the funds they invest will be used. This is why we accredit all our municipal investments as Green or Social loans.

We use the Green & Social Loan Principles to govern this process. These principles were developed by the Loan Market Association, along with other international finance associations, to strengthen the integrity of the market by providing a consistent framework. The UK Government has adopted the same approach, using the same Green Loan Principles to underpin its Green Gilt programme.

For municipal investments, verification and accreditation is performed by Abundance. We have designed the process to work within councils’ standard operating model, and it has been reviewed by the Loan Market Association for general compliance.

The four core components of accreditation

Use of funds

  • The cornerstone of accreditation is how funds will be used.
  • Intended projects must fall within a pre-determined list of eligible categories.
  • Projects may be new, or a re-finance of an existing project delivered 12 months prior to launch date of the loan.

Project evaluation and selection

  • Any project in a council’s capital programme can be funded if it falls within one of the defined categories.
  • The council should clearly communicate its process for evaluating projects.
  • The council should also describe the project’s green, social or sustainable objectives.

Management of funds

  • Funds should be tracked by the council in an appropriate manner.
  • The tracking should be documented using a formal internal process.
  • Documentation must link the funds raised to delivery of the eligible projects.

Reporting and verification

  • Councils should provide regular reports to investors via Abundance.
  • Reports should explain how funds have been used.
  • Where possible, reports should include project details, amounts allocated and expected impact.

Setting up and running a programme

Our aim is to make it as easy as possible for you to run your citizen investment programme, fitting within your existing ways of working. Our tried and tested processes and templated documents will help get everything you need in place to launch your first loan.

We’ll support you in creating engaging plans to communicate the investment to residents. Our investment platform makes all the administration easy, from paying returns to issuing updates.

Setup and approval

  • Updating treasury management strategy

  • Agree Key Terms

  • Agree eligible use of funds
  • Set the rate

Communications

  • Support in communications planning
  • Financial Promotion training
  • Create content in partnership

Monitoring

  • Abundance requests fund deployment updates
  • Ensure compliance with loan conditions

Loan administration

  • Regular investor updates
  • Investor interest and capital repayments

Three principles for growing your resident investor community

Think long term

It takes time to build awareness. A 5-year raise target with a programme of loans will boost confidence that this is a long-term initiative, not a one-off, and worthy of consideration as part of citizens’ financial plans.

Plan regular communications

It is important to let citizens know about your programme. Our experience suggests the best strategy for awareness is regular mentions planned across a variety of different communication channels.

Consider project storytelling

Strong storytelling through project selection and ongoing reporting helps to build engagement with citizens. You can focus on landmark projects, but we find it is equally valuable to shine a light on the initiatives that may go unnoticed by citizens. So far, we have found that either approach can be effective.
