GFI technical report / University of Leeds report

New research shows the scale of the municipal investment opportunity

A new technical report from the Green Finance Institute and the University of Leeds sets out the financial case for Community Municipal Investments as a credible addition to the local authority borrowing toolkit.

The report examines how the model has performed to date, how it compares with PWLB borrowing, and what it could deliver if adopted more widely by councils across the UK.

Read the full report

Key findings

Value for money

89%

The research found CMIs would have delivered savings for councils on around 89% of borrowing days over the past decade.

Proven discount

29bps

Councils have raised over £30m through CMIs at an average 29 basis point discount to the PWLB Certainty Rate after fees.

Market potential

£2.3bn

With the current tenor range, CMIs could meet up to 42% of medium-term borrowing demand from actively borrowing councils.

Sector savings

£76m

At scale, the report suggests the model could generate annual savings for the sector of up to £76m.

“Community Municipal Investments are a credible, lower-cost complement to PWLB. At the same time, CMIs provide diversification away from a single funding source and open up access to a deep pool of citizen and values-aligned institutional capital.”

“Over time, CMIs have the potential to become a long-term, citizen-backed source of affordable finance for local government. NS&I has been transformative for central government over the last 160 years, and this model offers a clear pathway to deliver similar benefits at the local level.”

Miles Ashton, Associate Director, Green Finance Institute

Understanding the wider value of citizen investment

A companion report from the University of Leeds explores the non-financial value created by Community Municipal Investments, looking at how the model changes the relationship between councils, citizens and the places they share.

The research focuses on what happens when residents are invited to participate directly in local delivery. It shows how municipal investments can help people feel more connected to their community, more aware of council activity and more confident that local government is delivering visible change.

“Municipal investments create a different kind of relationship between councils and residents. They give people a tangible stake in the future of their place, and that changes how local delivery is understood.”

Professor Mark Davis, University of Leeds

For councils, this points to a wider civic opportunity. The investment itself raises capital, but the communication around it can also build understanding: what is being funded, why it matters and how residents can see the benefit over time.

“The value of this model is not only financial. It helps create a shared civic mission, where citizens can see themselves as participants in making their places better.”

University of Leeds research team
