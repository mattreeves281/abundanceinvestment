Reinventing municipal investing for the digital age

It used to be common for UK councils to borrow money from their residents as an alternative to institutions or the government.

Throughout history, regional stock exchanges and municipal bonds have shaped and strengthened places across the UK; in cities such as Manchester, Liverpool, Glasgow and Bristol.

Municipal bonds have been used as far back as the 1500s to finance local infrastructure. These allowed councils to fund railway expansions, water supply systems and electric grids, while citizens appreciated the secure investment with a tangible link to their local place. At the start of the twentieth century, as much as 20% of council borrowing came from municipal bonds.

It is on these foundations that our own municipal investments are built - once again offering anyone the opportunity to become an investor in the future of the place they call home. In this era of financial markets and opaque cryptocurrencies, we want to connect people with what their money is really doing.

Bristol’s municipal investment history

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Bristol Council issued bonds to expand its port, improve roads and develop public utilities. These bonds attracted local investors, who saw the win-win of both financial returns and a tangible impact on their city’s growth. Some perpetual bonds issued in the 1940s are still paying returns today.

Creating a new generation of citizen investors

For generations, the idea of being an investor in a town or city was commonplace. Thousands of ordinary people - many of whom were unable to open bank accounts - were able to save for their future with their local authority. The benefits were obvious; making a return to help fund their families' futures while at the same time helping create the local civic infrastructure that made the places they lived function properly.

These citizen investors long predated the outdated stereotypes that we associate with investors today. These were ordinary people who wanted to take control of their financial futures and make the places they live better. And now our municipal investments are making it possible for citizens to invest in their places once again.

Creating a local NS&I

Over the last 160 years, the national Government has steadily built a way for people to invest in government activity. National Savings & Investments (NS&I) underpins the nation’s finances, providing 8% of Government borrowing. Millions of citizens save and invest through premium bonds and its other financial products.

We want to create an NS&I for local government, to let citizens take the central role in place-based finance that they once did - and share the benefits.

Invest in the foundations of our communities

Councils are the bedrocks of our places. They have a wide range of responsibilities to look after our places today and for the future. In fact, councils have over 1,200 statutory responsibilities - ranging from collecting our bins, through to providing social care in the community and housing.

And that’s before you look at their responsibility to invest in the future of places they serve. We need public infrastructure that is ready for the social and environmental changes that will come over the next decades. Whether that is tackling climate change, or ensuring a bright future for local businesses, councils need to make sure our places work for the long term.

Much of this essential work goes unnoticed. But can you imagine if the kind of society we would have if our councils weren’t there? If we didn’t ensure that the elderly had the support they need in their homes, or relied on the polluters to clean up their own waste?

Local government is at the centre of our lives in the UK, and we are proud to have created investments that let any citizen invest to support councils in delivering their long term plans.

How municipal lending works

Councils have two primary types of outgoings: day-to-day spending - like social care, education, waste management - and long term capital investment - like green and social infrastructure, housing and transport. Up to 20% of council budgets are typically capital investment.

There is a large amount of capital investment each year: on average, councils have borrowed around £9 billion a year from the Public Works Loan Board, which is the Government backed institution that is usually used for this type of borrowing.

Our municipal loans are another way for councils to borrow money for these project investments. Our vision is to create a future where more and more of these funds come from engaged citizens with a real stake in the future of their communities.
