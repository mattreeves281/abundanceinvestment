How it works

Municipal investments are low-risk loans to UK councils. Councils use these funds to improve the local area, while offering you a competitive and stable long-term income.

Benefits at a glance

Our municipal investments are long-term and low-risk. They’re similar to other low-risk investments, such as NS&I and fixed rate savings products (learn more about how they compare). And they are ISA eligible, meaning you can invest through an Innovative Finance ISA for tax-free returns.

They offer competitive rates of return, letting your money keep pace with the rising cost of living, and they pay fixed cash interest payments every six months across the investment term, which is usually 5 years. See how the returns work.

Our flexible approach lets you choose how you want to invest. You can pick your own investments, and decide how you use those regular interest payments: either reinvest in new municipal investments to keep growing your money, or withdraw for free to spend.

Or you can let our automatic investment option keep your money invested and working to creating an income for you while improving the places we live and work.

How our municipal investments compare

Our municipal investments are a good option for savers and investors looking to create a long term low-risk income who also want to see their money making a positive difference. If you are looking to compare the features of our investments to other low-risk options, here are some things you should think about.

Municipal investments vs fixed rate deposits / bank saving bonds

1

Investments are different from savings

Fixed rate deposits and bank bonds are deposit products with FSCS protection. You do not get FSCS protection with our municipal investments. It is also important to remember that investment involves some risks, so make sure you understand these before you proceed.

2

Similar financial features

Our municipal investments offer similar features to a fixed rate deposits and bank saving bonds. All these options pay returns which are fixed over the investment term, and you have tax free ISA options available.

3

Different ways to exit early

With municipal investments you have the option to list your investment for sale using our marketplace, although a sale is not guaranteed. Fixed rate deposits may allow withdrawals but many don’t and the exact terms vary from one product to the next. Bank savings bonds typically have to be held for the full term.

4

See what your money is doing

You can see directly what your money is funding when you invest with us. This is rarely possible on deposit products like bank savings bonds and fixed term deposits.

Municipal investments vs National Savings & Investments (NS&I)

1

Lending to government

NS&I offer a range of saving and investment products, backed by the Treasury. When you use NS&I you are lending money to the national government. With municipal investments, you are also lending to the public sector - but in this case it is local government.

2

Both are low risk options

For both options the risk of losing your money is very low. NS&I is backed by the Treasury, and municipal investments are backed by councils. We discuss this topic in more detail here.

3

Municipal investments have some unique benefits

Our municipal investments offer some distinct financial benefits over NS&I. The investments are ISA eligible, pay regular cash interest payments and you can sell before maturity if you need to.

Automatic investment

Automatic investment is a convenient option for investors who want to make sure their money is always working to earn returns and to help deliver positive change.

When you enable automatic investment for your account, it means that any money you deposit will be split evenly across open investments. It is up to you whether you deposit using a regular standing order, or make one-off deposits when it suits you. When you receive investment returns, these will also be reinvested in the same way.

And it is simple to turn off automatic investment in your account. So if you want to start withdrawing your returns as cash, rather than reinvesting them, that is easy to do.
