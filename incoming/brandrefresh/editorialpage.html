Greening the grey in Hammersmith & Fulham

Hammersmith & Fulham is now on its third Green Investment with Abundance, having already raised over £2 million from its first two offers. The council has put some of the earlier proceeds to work to expand its ‘greening the grey’ programme. This initiative focuses on transforming hard, grey spaces into vibrant green areas, enhancing biodiversity, improving climate resilience and making the borough a more pleasant place to live and work.

Rain gardens across the borough

Part of this involves the creation of rainwater gardens which add greenery to the local streetscape while creating natural flood defences and supporting biodiversity. These are being rolled out across the area, for example in:

  • King Street in Hammersmith, providing an attractive natural barrier between the road and the shops
  • Purcell Crescent in Fulham, where approximately 400 square metres of tarmac have been removed to make way for sustainable drainage, trees and a new path
  • Edith Road in Hammersmith where four rain gardens are being created, turning unused highway space into natural drainage systems

Nature returning to shared spaces

A host of other exciting efforts to bring nature into the city include the transformation of Eelbrook Common from concrete paddling pool to green sanctuary. Formerly a magnet for anti-social behaviour, the area was replanted by local volunteers last Spring and is now a lush natural oasis and a safer, more welcoming space for local people.

Community investment in action

£400k was used last year to make a new community garden in Frank Banfield Park, which includes the borough’s fourth Tiny Forest, a wildflower meadow and an accessible toilet made from recycled shipping containers. A gang of local volunteers was recruited to do most of the planting and the new area will be kept looking lovely thanks to proceeds of the Green Investment.

These projects are great examples of how this raised through Hammersmith & Fulham’s Green Investments are engaging the community directly in building greener, more resilient urban environments.
