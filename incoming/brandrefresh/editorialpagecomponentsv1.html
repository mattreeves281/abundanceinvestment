Comparison

Two ways to approach the same outcome

Some readers want control over individual choices. Others want a more regular, lower-effort route. This block helps explain the difference without shifting out of editorial mode.

Option one

Choose individual investments

  • You can select specific offers that match your interests
  • You see each investment as a separate decision
  • This may suit readers who value visibility and choice
  • It can take more active comparison and decision-making

Option two

Use a regular investing route

  • You make one initial choice, then contribute on a repeating basis
  • The process can feel simpler and easier to maintain
  • This may suit readers who prefer convenience over active selection
  • You may feel less connected to each individual underlying choice

Useful for explainers, gateway pages, and any point where a reader needs help understanding two legitimate paths rather than a “right answer”.

Self-selection

Is this right for you?

This component helps readers decide whether to continue, without forcing the answer. It is especially useful on product explainers and high-intent educational pages.

May suit you if…

  • You want a clearer link between your money and real-world outcomes
  • You are comfortable reading a little more before making a decision
  • You value structure, explanation and visible next steps
  • You are comparing more than one possible route

May be less suitable if…

  • You want to make a decision with minimal reading or comparison
  • You are looking for a product with no investment risk
  • You prefer highly short-term access to your money
  • You need certainty rather than a balance of return and impact
This works well just before a CTA, because it helps the reader identify themselves before deciding what to do next.

FAQ

Common questions

A compact accordion is useful when the page needs to answer practical objections without overloading the main narrative.

How is this different from an ordinary savings account?

A savings account is typically designed around capital security and access. An investment product usually involves different risk, return and time-horizon considerations.

Do I need to understand every financial term before continuing?

Not necessarily. This system can pair FAQs with glossary or definition blocks so readers can get the level of explanation they need without leaving the page.

Where should risk information sit in relation to these components?

Usually adjacent to the decision-support area or just before a CTA. It should feel easy to find, not buried beneath promotional copy.

Can these components also work on council and explainer pages?

Yes. They are designed as insertable editorial blocks, so they can sit inside articles, explainers, place-based pages and decision-support content without changing the page’s overall rhythm.

Glossary

Plain-language financial definitions

This can sit beside FAQs and decision-support blocks to stop the reader hitting jargon walls.

Rate of return
The amount an investment is expected to pay over time, usually shown as a yearly percentage.
Capital at risk
A reminder that the money invested is not guaranteed and could be worth less than the amount put in.
Term length
The period over which the investment is intended to run before final repayment.
Capital repayment
How and when the original amount invested is returned — for example gradually or at the end of the term.

Expandable detail

More detail without overloading the main page

Useful for additional practical explanation, worked examples or edge-case information.

How might repayments work in practice?

Some investments return capital only at the end of the term, while others return part of the capital at set intervals during the life of the investment.

Why do term and liquidity need to be explained separately?

A product can have a fixed term and still differ in how easily an investor can access their money before that term ends. Readers often conflate these ideas.

When should this component be used instead of an FAQ?

Use expandable detail when the content is supplementary depth, rather than a common objection or direct question the majority of readers are likely to have.

Important to check

A checklist-style warning panel

This version is useful when the page needs to highlight several cautionary factors rather than one headline statement.

1

Time horizon matters

Some investment products are designed to be held over a fixed period, which may not suit readers who need quick access to their money.

2

Risk and return should be read together

A higher quoted return may be meaningful only when understood alongside the level of risk and the conditions attached to the offer.

3

Readers may need the full offer document

Editorial pages can explain and guide, but some decisions still require the reader to consult the detailed product documentation.
